The Art of the Solo Travel Meetup: How to Find Your People in Any City

2026-05-30 · Community · Tags: solotravelguide, The

There's a moment every solo traveler knows too well: you've just checked into your accommodation, the sun is setting, and you're standing in a foreign city with a full evening ahead and no one to share it with. The freedom is exhilarating, but sometimes the silence can feel heavy. While solo travel is about self-discovery and independence, it doesn't have to mean isolation. In fact, the most transformative journeys often happen when you connect with others along the way. Building a community on the road isn't just about avoiding loneliness—it's about enriching your experience, gaining local insights, and creating memories that last far beyond the trip itself. This guide will show you how to master the art of the solo travel meetup, turning strangers into friends and cities into homes away from home.

Why Community Matters for Solo Travelers

Traveling alone doesn't mean you have to be alone. The solo travel community is a vibrant, global network of like-minded adventurers who share a passion for exploration. Connecting with this community offers more than just companionship; it provides a safety net, a source of local knowledge, and a support system that can make all the difference when you're far from home. When you meet fellow travelers, you exchange tips about hidden gems, warn each other about tourist traps, and share the kind of spontaneous laughter that turns a good trip into an unforgettable one. Moreover, community can be a practical tool: traveling with a new friend for a day or two can reduce costs, increase safety in numbers, and open doors to experiences you'd never find alone. But beyond the logistics, there's a deeper benefit—feeling a sense of belonging in a world that often feels vast and impersonal. That connection is what turns a solo journey into a shared human experience.

The Hostel Effect: More Than Just a Bed

Hostels have long been the epicenter of solo travel social life, and for good reason. They're designed to foster interaction, from communal kitchens to common rooms with board games and movie nights. But not all hostels are created equal. To maximize your chances of meeting people, look for hostels that advertise social events—pub crawls, walking tours, family dinners, or even yoga sessions. Many hostels have a bulletin board or a WhatsApp group where you can see what's happening that day. Don't be shy about introducing yourself in the common area; a simple "Hey, where are you from?" can spark a conversation that leads to a day of exploring together. If you're not staying in a hostel, you can still tap into this network by visiting hostel bars or attending their public events. Many hostels welcome non-guests for their social activities, especially if you're just looking for a friendly face. Remember, everyone in a hostel is in the same boat—they're all travelers looking for connection, so the ice is already thin.

Digital Nomad Hubs and Co-Working Spaces

If you're traveling while working remotely, co-working spaces are your golden ticket to community. These spaces are magnets for digital nomads, freelancers, and remote workers who are often just as eager to socialize as you are. Many co-working spaces host weekly happy hours, skill-sharing sessions, or networking events. Even if you're not a remote worker, you can still drop by a co-working space to work on your travel blog or catch up on emails—the atmosphere is usually more social than a coffee shop. Look for spaces that have a community manager; they're often the hub of information about local events and can introduce you to other members. Some cities have entire neighborhoods known for their digital nomad scene, like Chiang Mai's Nimman area or Lisbon's Cais do Sodré. By positioning yourself in these hubs, you'll naturally find yourself surrounded by people who understand the solo travel lifestyle and are open to making new friends.

Meetup Apps and Local Events: The Modern Matchmaker

Technology has made it easier than ever to find your people on the road. Apps like Meetup, Couchsurfing, and even Facebook Groups are treasure troves of local events and gatherings. Meetup.com is particularly useful for finding interest-based groups—whether you're into hiking, photography, language exchange, or board games, there's likely a group in your city. Couchsurfing isn't just for free accommodation; its events section lists everything from city tours to potluck dinners, and the community is famously welcoming to travelers. Another great option is the app "Hostelworld" which has a social feature, or "Backpackr" which connects travelers in real time. When you arrive in a new city, spend 15 minutes browsing these platforms and you'll likely find at least one event happening that evening. Don't be afraid to attend alone—everyone else is there to meet people too. The key is to be proactive: message the organizer or a few attendees beforehand to let them know you're coming. That way, you'll have a familiar face when you walk in.

Group Tours and Day Trips: Structured Socializing

Sometimes the best way to meet people is to let someone else do the planning. Group tours and day trips are perfect for solo travelers who want to socialize without the pressure of initiating every conversation. Whether it's a food tour, a hiking excursion, or a historical walking tour, you'll be surrounded by other travelers who share your interest. The shared experience of discovering a new place creates an instant bond. Many tour companies specifically cater to solo travelers, offering small group sizes and a social atmosphere. For example, companies like G Adventures and Intrepid Travel have "solo-friendly" tours that include free time for independent exploration but also have built-in group activities. Even a simple free walking tour can lead to meeting people—after the tour, suggest grabbing a coffee or a drink with a few of the group members. The key is to be open and approachable. Smile, ask questions, and show genuine interest in your fellow travelers. You'll be surprised how quickly a group of strangers becomes a temporary family.

Nurturing Connections Beyond the Trip

The beauty of solo travel community is that it doesn't have to end when you board your flight home. In today's connected world, the friends you make on the road can become lifelong companions. Exchange social media handles or WhatsApp numbers with the people you click with. Create a shared photo album or a group chat to keep the memories alive. Many travelers find that they reunite with their travel friends in different cities months or even years later. The solo travel community is a global network, and the more you nurture these connections, the richer your future travels will be. You might even find yourself hosting a fellow traveler in your own city, returning the kindness you received on the road. Beyond that, online communities like the SoloTravelGuide forum or various Facebook groups allow you to stay engaged with the community even when you're not traveling. Share your stories, ask for advice, and offer support to others. This sense of belonging can be a powerful antidote to the post-trip blues, and it keeps the spirit of adventure alive in your everyday life.

Conclusion

Mastering the art of the solo travel meetup is not about being extroverted or having a perfect itinerary—it's about being open to the unexpected connections that make travel so magical. Whether you find your people in a hostel common room, a co-working space, a meetup app, or a group tour, the key is to take that first step. Say yes to the invitation, strike up a conversation, and let the community embrace you. Remember, every solo traveler you meet is on their own journey, and the shared experience of navigating the unknown creates a bond that transcends borders. So next time you're in a new city, don't just see the sights—connect with the souls. Your travel community is waiting for you, and it might just change the way you see the world.
